### Post 1

Jay Acunzo has talked for a long time about "resonance over reach." And I agree with him. Don't just chase impressions. Don't optimize every idea for the biggest possible audience. Don't water down your work until it's broadly legible but mostly forgettable.Reach without resonance is just noise.But for marketers, we don't get to opt out of reach.I think what we really need is sequencing — resonance BEFORE reach. Make the thing specific enough to matter, spread, and be remembered. Our work still has to travel, but it travels further when it actually means something to someone first.

### Post 2

"How would you start a marketing strategy from scratch?"The annoying but true answer is: it depends. But because that's less fun, I would start with one question: How is this business supposed to grow?Is the intended growth mechanism raw discovery? Meaning: people have a problem, they search for it, and we need to show up. Cool. Then SEO, comparison pages, and educational content probably matter a lot.Is it product-led growth? Meaning: users become a force multiplier, and they ultimately need to share the product with others or use the product more frequently in order to gain more value? Then onboarding content, use-case content, templates, lifecycle emails, and activation loops probably matter more.Is it sales-led? Meaning: the business depends on reps reaching out, building relationships, educating prospects, and moving accounts through a long buying process. Then the first content priority might be the sales asset the team has been begging for.After this, I would look for the shortest path to proving marketing can help. Early on, marketing needs to earn internal trust. The fastest way to do that is to understand how the company grows, then make that motion work better.

### Post 3

"What's our AI visibility like?" If my CEO or CMO asked me that, here's what I'd do. First, run our brand through HubSpot's AEO Grader. Then summarize what stands out.I did this for SparkToro. The report correctly identified us as a niche audience research tool. And it picked up on one of our biggest advantages: credibility with marketing practitioners.Other parts need a human read: The competitive set is messy. SparkToro gets compared to Semrush, Ahrefs, Similarweb, Brandwatch, and more. Some are fair nearby alternatives. They're not all direct competitors. But this info is useful because it shows how AI is categorizing the brand, even when it gets that wrong.So here's what I'd do: Find what's accurate. Find the misconceptions. Turn the gaps into priorities. That's a to-do list for website copy, content, PR, sales enablement, and category education. Then run this analysis again in 1-2 months to see what moved.
