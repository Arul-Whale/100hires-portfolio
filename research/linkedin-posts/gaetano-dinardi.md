### Post 2

Nearly all of the SEO/GEO problems I am working on today are category misalignment and mismatch problems... not ticky-tacky "optimization" tricks or GEO hacks.B2B SaaS brand that's been around for 10+ years has a digital footprint in a "legacy" category that's now unsexy... but it's still their breadwinner.And now there's a new thing they need to become known for:Customer Service CRM to Intelligent CX Platform. Low Code Platform to Agentic Systems Platform.I've seen companies setup Profound with a massive list of LLM prompt tracking. The dashboards show zero visibility and they wonder why. The problem is that AI retrieval often follows entity neighborhoods. So when SaaS brands evolve into new positioning, they need a FULL BLOWN category attack plan with 50% of the effort allocated to on-site content and the other 50% allocated to offsite: analyst relations, guest posts, review site optimization, YouTube, inclusion on Forrester/Gartner reports for the new category. Instead, they launch one new product page with a press release and expect magic to happen. It just doesn't work.

### Post 3

One of the hardest things about "traffic going down" is managing the executive perception of it.Take this scenario: organic traffic is down 15% May vs April, but demo requests attributed to organic search are down by 30%.They will assume: Something is wrong. Traffic is down. And as a result, we are losing leads. But here's what no one considered: The drop in demo requests is a normalization after a few unusually strong months. Overall website traffic was the same. The channel mix is what actually shifted. Organic was affected by a strong paid search month capturing demand that otherwise would have been attributed to organic. The biggest traffic declines were mostly from TOFU informational pages, not high-intent commercial pages. AI search leads doubled.It's on YOU, as the channel owner, to surface these insights, control the narrative, and manage the executive perception.If you don't, then good luck.
